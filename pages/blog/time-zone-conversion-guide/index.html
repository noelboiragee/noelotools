Time Zone Conversion: Avoiding Scheduling Confusion

Learn about UTC offsets, daylight saving changes and the details to check when arranging meetings across countries.

NoeloTools Editorial•6 min read

Scheduling a call across countries can be confusing because local clocks do not always move together. Time-zone abbreviations and daylight saving rules can also change the difference between two locations during the year.

Understand UTC offsets

UTC is a common reference for coordinating time. A location's UTC offset describes how far its local time is ahead of or behind UTC at a particular date. Some locations use a different offset during part of the year.

Why daylight saving time matters

Some regions move clocks seasonally and others do not. As a result, the time difference between two cities can change during the year. Do not assume that a time difference observed today will apply months later.

Use a city and a date

When converting a meeting time, select the actual city or time zone and include the meeting date. This helps account for daylight saving rules where they apply.

Be careful with abbreviations

Short labels such as CST can refer to different time zones in different regions. Prefer a named city or a full time-zone identifier when sending an invitation.

Confirm the calendar invitation

Include the date, city or time zone and intended local time. Calendar invitations that store a precise time can display it in each attendee's local zone.

Practical reminder

For important meetings, verify the converted time in a second calendar or time-zone reference and include the time zone in the invitation.
