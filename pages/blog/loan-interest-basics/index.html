Loan Interest Explained: Rate, Term and Monthly Payments

Understand the basic parts of a loan calculation and why the total repayment can exceed the amount borrowed.

NoeloTools Editorial•7 min read

A loan's monthly payment depends on more than the amount borrowed. The interest rate, repayment period and fee structure all affect what a borrower pays over time. Understanding these parts helps you compare offers more carefully.

Principal and interest

The principal is the amount borrowed. Interest is the cost charged for borrowing it. A repayment normally covers both, although the split between principal and interest can change over the loan term.

How the interest rate matters

A higher rate generally increases the borrowing cost when other terms are unchanged. Check whether the quoted rate is fixed or variable, and whether it is an annual nominal rate or an effective annual rate.

Why the loan term matters

A longer term can reduce the scheduled monthly payment, but it often means paying interest for more months. A shorter term may increase each payment while reducing total interest, depending on the contract.

Compare the full cost

Review the total amount repayable, fees, payment frequency, early repayment conditions and whether the rate can change. Do not compare loans using monthly payment alone.

Estimate a payment

The Loan Calculator can provide an estimate when you enter the principal, interest rate and term. Treat the result as an illustration; lender calculations and fees may differ.

Practical reminder

A calculator estimate is not a loan offer. Read the lender's terms and confirm all fees and rate conditions before making a financial decision.
