Germany model: tax year 2026

This is a simplified estimate for 2026. The implementation uses a basic tax-free allowance of €12,348, an annual work-expense allowance of €1,230, and estimated contribution ceilings of €5,812.50 per month for health and long-term care insurance and €8,450 per month for pension and unemployment insurance.

  • Pension insurance: employee share 9.3% up to the pension contribution ceiling.
  • Unemployment insurance: employee share 1.3% up to the pension contribution ceiling.
  • Health insurance: employee share is half of 14.6% plus the entered insurer additional rate, up to the health contribution ceiling.
  • Long-term care insurance: estimated employee rate 1.8% for parents or 2.4% for childless people outside Saxony; 1.3% or 2.3% respectively in Saxony.
  • Income tax: simplified progressive 2026 formula using the tax brackets encoded in this calculator; church tax is applied at the selected 0%, 8% or 9% of estimated income tax.

The estimated taxable income subtracts the work-expense allowance and estimated employee pension, health and care contributions. It does not reproduce every official tax, allowance, surcharge, special case or payroll rule. Treat the output as an indicative estimate, not a payslip or tax filing calculation.

Brazil model: tax year 2026

The Brazil estimate uses the 2026 progressive employee INSS contribution table and the monthly IRPF withholding table, including the 2026 tax reduction for monthly taxable earnings up to BRL 7,350. It assumes one standard employment relationship, no dependants and the simplified monthly deduction. It does not include employer-side charges, the 13th salary, multiple employers or individual exemptions.

Official sources: INSS contribution table 2026 and Federal Revenue 2026 income-tax tables.

Spain model: 2026 employee withholding estimate

The Spain model follows the Spanish Tax Agency (AEAT) 2026 employment-income withholding algorithm for a single employee with no children or dependants, one standard indefinite employment contract, no disability, no other income and no housing-loan reduction. Gross monthly salary is annualised over 12 equal monthly equivalents, with extra payments treated as prorated. Employee social contributions use the 2026 General Regime rates: 4.70% common contingencies, 1.55% unemployment, 0.10% training and 0.15% MEI, subject to the €5,101.20 monthly contribution ceiling, plus the employee solidarity contribution on earnings above that ceiling. The withholding estimate uses the AEAT employment-expense deduction, Article 20 work-income reduction where eligible, the 2026 withholding scale and the €5,550 personal minimum. It is a payroll withholding estimate, not a final annual tax return; regional tax differences and individual circumstances can change final IRPF.

Official sources: AEAT — 2026 withholding calculation algorithm, Spanish Social Security — 2026 contribution rates, and BOE Order PJC/297/2026.

Italy model: 2026 employee estimate

The Italy estimate assumes a standard private-sector employee with a baseline 9.19% employee INPS contribution, one annual employment income, and no dependants. It applies 2026 national IRPEF bands (23%, 33%, 43%), the employee income-tax credit and the 2026 tax-wedge relief for eligible employees. Regional and municipal IRPEF surcharges, 13th/14th salary instalments, contract-specific contribution variations and individual deductions are excluded.

Sources: Italian Chamber of Deputies — 2026 IRPEF rates, Law 207/2024 — employee tax credits and tax-wedge relief, and INPS contribution table (ordinary employee IVS share).

France model: 2026 employee estimate

The France estimate assumes one employee, one tax share, no dependants and a standard private-sector non-managerial payroll. Employee deductions include capped and uncapped basic pension, AGIRC-ARRCO supplementary pension, CSG and CRDS. The calculation uses the published 2026 income-tax scale as an indicative reference and applies the standard 10% employment-expense allowance. Actual withholding depends on the tax household, personalised withholding rate, payroll status and later tax-year updates.

Official sources: CLEISS 2026 employee social contribution rates and Service-Public 2026 income-tax scale. The 2026 scale shown by Service-Public relates to 2025 income declarations; it is not a final 2026-income assessment.

United Kingdom model: tax year 2026–27

The UK estimate uses the 2026–27 standard Personal Allowance of £12,570, tapered by £1 for every £2 of adjusted income above £100,000, and the England, Wales and Northern Ireland income-tax bands: 20%, 40% and 45%. Employee Class 1 National Insurance uses the standard category A monthly thresholds of £1,048 and £4,189, at 8% and 2% respectively. The default region is England, Wales and Northern Ireland; Scottish income-tax bands are not included.

This is an annualised estimate from monthly gross salary. Actual PAYE can vary with tax code, pay frequency, pension contributions, benefits, student loans and cumulative payroll rounding. Official sources: HMRC Income Tax rates and Personal Allowances, HMRC 2026–27 rates and thresholds and HMRC employee National Insurance rates.

Belgium model: 2026 white-collar employee estimate

The Belgium estimate assumes a single resident white-collar employee with no dependants. Monthly gross salary is annualised over 12 months; holiday pay and year-end bonus are excluded. Employee ONSS is calculated at 13.07%, reduced by the 2026 work bonus using the indexed September 2026 non-manual employee thresholds. Taxable employment income is reduced by the standard professional-expense allowance of 30%, capped at €6,070. The federal tax calculation applies the 2026 income-tax brackets (25%, 40%, 45% and 50%) and the basic personal tax allowance of €11,550. The model excludes the municipality-specific local tax surcharge, special social-security contribution, regional tax reductions, family allowances and any sector-specific payroll variations; actual net pay can therefore differ.

Official sources: FPS Finance — 2026 income-tax brackets and personal allowance, FPS Finance — employee professional-expense deduction, 2026 work-bonus thresholds for non-manual employees.

Netherlands model: 2026 employee estimate

The Netherlands estimate assumes an employee below the AOW age, resident in the Netherlands for the full year, with one employment income and both the general tax credit and labour tax credit applied. It annualises monthly gross salary over 12 months and applies the 2026 Box 1 rates: 35.75% up to €38,883, 37.56% from €38,883 to €78,426, and 49.50% above €78,426. The first bracket combines income tax with national insurance premiums; these are included in the payroll-tax result rather than shown as a separate employee social-insurance deduction. The 2026 general tax credit and labour tax credit are calculated using the published income thresholds and phase-out rates. For a standard employee, the employer normally pays the income-related Zvw contribution (6.10% in 2026), so it is not deducted from take-home pay. Private basic health-insurance premiums are paid separately and are excluded.

Official sources: Belastingdienst — 2026 tax rates and tax credits, Belastingdienst — 2026 Zvw contribution rates.

Important information

Net salary depends on more than the country: tax year, tax class or filing status, region, age, children, health insurance, pension scheme and other deductions can all affect the result.

The available Germany, France, Italy, Spain, Netherlands, Belgium, Brazil and UK models are simplified estimates, not official payroll calculations. The other 22 country models are not yet enabled. Do not use this tool as tax, payroll or legal advice.

Cross-country methodology reference: OECD Taxing Wages 2026. Country-specific rules will be documented individually as each model is released.
