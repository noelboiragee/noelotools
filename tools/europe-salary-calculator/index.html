Germany model: tax year 2026

This is a simplified estimate for 2026. The implementation uses a basic tax-free allowance of €12,348, an annual work-expense allowance of €1,230, and estimated contribution ceilings of €5,812.50 per month for health and long-term care insurance and €8,450 per month for pension and unemployment insurance.

  • Pension insurance: employee share 9.3% up to the pension contribution ceiling.
  • Unemployment insurance: employee share 1.3% up to the pension contribution ceiling.
  • Health insurance: employee share is half of 14.6% plus the entered insurer additional rate, up to the health contribution ceiling.
  • Long-term care insurance: estimated employee rate 1.8% for parents or 2.4% for childless people outside Saxony; 1.3% or 2.3% respectively in Saxony.
  • Income tax: simplified progressive 2026 formula using the tax brackets encoded in this calculator; church tax is applied at the selected 0%, 8% or 9% of estimated income tax.

The estimated taxable income subtracts the work-expense allowance and estimated employee pension, health and care contributions. It does not reproduce every official tax, allowance, surcharge, special case or payroll rule. Treat the output as an indicative estimate, not a payslip or tax filing calculation.

Brazil model: tax year 2026

The Brazil estimate uses the 2026 progressive employee INSS contribution table and the monthly IRPF withholding table, including the 2026 tax reduction for monthly taxable earnings up to BRL 7,350. It assumes one standard employment relationship, no dependants and the simplified monthly deduction. It does not include employer-side charges, the 13th salary, multiple employers or individual exemptions.

Official sources: INSS contribution table 2026 and Federal Revenue 2026 income-tax tables.

Spain model: 2026 employee withholding estimate

The Spain model follows the Spanish Tax Agency (AEAT) 2026 employment-income withholding algorithm for a single employee with no children or dependants, one standard indefinite employment contract, no disability, no other income and no housing-loan reduction. Gross monthly salary is annualised over 12 equal monthly equivalents, with extra payments treated as prorated. Employee social contributions use the 2026 General Regime rates: 4.70% common contingencies, 1.55% unemployment, 0.10% training and 0.15% MEI, subject to the €5,101.20 monthly contribution ceiling, plus the employee solidarity contribution on earnings above that ceiling. The withholding estimate uses the AEAT employment-expense deduction, Article 20 work-income reduction where eligible, the 2026 withholding scale and the €5,550 personal minimum. It is a payroll withholding estimate, not a final annual tax return; regional tax differences and individual circumstances can change final IRPF.

Official sources: AEAT — 2026 withholding calculation algorithm, Spanish Social Security — 2026 contribution rates, and BOE Order PJC/297/2026.

Italy model: 2026 employee estimate

The Italy estimate assumes a standard private-sector employee with a baseline 9.19% employee INPS contribution, one annual employment income, and no dependants. It applies 2026 national IRPEF bands (23%, 33%, 43%), the employee income-tax credit and the 2026 tax-wedge relief for eligible employees. Regional and municipal IRPEF surcharges, 13th/14th salary instalments, contract-specific contribution variations and individual deductions are excluded.

Sources: Italian Chamber of Deputies — 2026 IRPEF rates, Law 207/2024 — employee tax credits and tax-wedge relief, and INPS contribution table (ordinary employee IVS share).

France model: 2026 employee estimate

The France estimate assumes one employee, one tax share, no dependants and a standard private-sector non-managerial payroll. Employee deductions include capped and uncapped basic pension, AGIRC-ARRCO supplementary pension, CSG and CRDS. The calculation uses the published 2026 income-tax scale as an indicative reference and applies the standard 10% employment-expense allowance. Actual withholding depends on the tax household, personalised withholding rate, payroll status and later tax-year updates.

Official sources: CLEISS 2026 employee social contribution rates and Service-Public 2026 income-tax scale. The 2026 scale shown by Service-Public relates to 2025 income declarations; it is not a final 2026-income assessment.

United Kingdom model: tax year 2026–27

The UK estimate uses the 2026–27 standard Personal Allowance of £12,570, tapered by £1 for every £2 of adjusted income above £100,000, and the England, Wales and Northern Ireland income-tax bands: 20%, 40% and 45%. Employee Class 1 National Insurance uses the standard category A monthly thresholds of £1,048 and £4,189, at 8% and 2% respectively. The default region is England, Wales and Northern Ireland; Scottish income-tax bands are not included.

This is an annualised estimate from monthly gross salary. Actual PAYE can vary with tax code, pay frequency, pension contributions, benefits, student loans and cumulative payroll rounding. Official sources: HMRC Income Tax rates and Personal Allowances, HMRC 2026–27 rates and thresholds and HMRC employee National Insurance rates.

Switzerland model: 2026 Zurich benchmark

The Switzerland estimate uses a single employee resident in Zurich city, no church tax, and 12 equal monthly salary payments. Employee deductions include AHV/IV/EO at 5.30%, ALV at 1.10% up to CHF 148,200, non-occupational accident insurance at an assumed 1.00%, and an assumed 6.50% occupational pension contribution on coordinated salary (CHF 26,460 deduction; CHF 90,720 upper limit). Federal taxable income includes the standard work-expense deduction (3%, CHF 2,000–4,000) and CHF 1,800 insurance-premium deduction. Zurich taxable income includes the 10% work-expense deduction (CHF 6,600–12,400) and CHF 2,900 insurance-premium deduction. Federal tax uses the 2026 single-person schedule; cantonal tax uses the Zurich 2026 single-person tariff multiplied by the 95% cantonal and 119% city multipliers. Private health-insurance premiums paid by the employee, wealth tax, church tax and plan-specific deviations are not deducted from net pay. Other cantons and municipalities can produce materially different results.

Official sources: Canton of Zurich — 2026 cantonal and municipal tax multipliers, Canton of Zurich — 2026 single-person tax tariff and inflation adjustment, Federal Tax Administration — 2026 federal income-tax rates, Federal Social Insurance Office — 2026 employee contribution rates, Zurich Tax Office — 2026 payroll deductions and contribution assumptions.

Sweden model: 2026 employee estimate

The Sweden estimate assumes a working-age full-year resident employee, using the 2026 national-average municipal income-tax rate of 32.38%. It uses the official 2026 basic-allowance table, 20% state income tax on taxable earned income above SEK 643,000, the earned-income tax credit, and the 0.75% general tax reduction on taxable income above SEK 40,000 (capped at SEK 1,500). A 0.292% funeral fee is included; Church of Sweden fee, municipality-specific rate differences and other personal deductions are excluded. The 7% public pension contribution is normally fully offset by a corresponding tax reduction and is therefore not shown as a separate net deduction.

Official sources: Skatteverket — 2026 tax rates, average municipal tax and pension-fee credit, Skatteverket — 2026 basic allowance table, OECD — 2026 earned-income tax credit methodology.

Denmark model: 2026 employee estimate

The Denmark estimate assumes a full-time employee with one salary income, no church tax and no private pension deduction. It applies the 8% labour-market contribution after a standard ATP employee payment of DKK 99 per month, the 12.01% bottom-bracket tax, 7.5% middle-bracket tax above DKK 641,200 after AM, 7.5% top-bracket tax above DKK 777,900 and 5% additional top-bracket tax above DKK 2,592,700. The personal allowance is DKK 54,100. Employment allowance is 12.75% up to DKK 63,471 and job allowance is 4.5% above DKK 235,200 up to DKK 3,100. Municipal tax uses the 2026 national average of 25.049%; the 44.57% tax ceiling is applied to ordinary state and municipal income tax. Church tax and personal deductions are excluded.

Official sources: Skattestyrelsen — 8% labour-market contribution, Skattestyrelsen — 2026 state tax brackets, Skattestyrelsen — employment and job allowances, Skattestyrelsen — 2026 personal allowance and tax rates, and PwC — 2026 average municipal tax rate.

Norway model: 2026 employee estimate

The Norway estimate assumes a single full-year tax resident outside the northern Action Zone, with salary as the only income. It applies 22% ordinary-income tax after the 2026 minimum standard deduction (46%, capped at NOK 95,700) and personal allowance (NOK 114,540), plus 2026 bracket tax thresholds of NOK 226,100, 318,300, 725,050, 980,100 and 1,467,200 at rates of 1.7%, 4.0%, 13.7%, 16.8% and 17.8%. Employee National Insurance is 7.6%, subject to the NOK 99,650 lower limit and the statutory 25% cap on the portion above that limit. Employer National Insurance, wealth tax, additional deductions and the reduced 18.5% northern Action Zone rate are excluded.

Official sources: Norwegian Tax Administration — 2026 tax rates, allowances and deductions, National Insurance contribution rate and lower limit, Minimum standard deduction.

Uruguay model: 2026 resident employee estimate

The Uruguay estimate assumes a resident employee with no spouse or dependent children, 13 salary payments per year (12 regular salaries plus aguinaldo), and a monthly salary under the pension contribution cap. It applies the 2026 BPC of UYU 6,864, progressive annual IRPF bands from 84 to 1,380 BPC and the applicable marginal rates of 10%, 15%, 24%, 25%, 27%, 31% and 36%. Employee social contributions include 15% retirement, FONASA at 3% up to 2.5 BPC or 4.5% above that threshold for a single worker without dependants, and 0.1% FRL. Social contribution deductions receive a 14% or 8% deduction rate depending on annual income. The estimate excludes additional family deductions, other income, salary-specific caps and employer contributions.

Official sources: IMPO — Decree 11/026 setting 2026 BPC, Ministry of Economy and Finance — 2026 IRPF annual income and deduction scales, BPS — FONASA employee contribution rates, BPS — 15% employee pension contribution.

South Korea model: 2026 resident employee estimate

The South Korea estimate assumes a resident employee with one workplace-based job, no dependants and no additional tax credits. It applies the 2026 earned-income deduction (capped at KRW 20 million), the KRW 1.5 million basic personal deduction, and the progressive national income-tax rates of 6%, 15%, 24%, 35%, 38%, 40%, 42% and 45%, plus local income tax at 10% of national income tax. Employee social contributions include National Pension at 4.75% up to the KRW 6.59 million monthly income ceiling, health insurance at 3.595%, long-term care at 0.4724%, and employment insurance at 0.9%. The model excludes detailed year-end tax credits, dependants, non-taxable benefits, employer contributions and individual adjustments.

Official sources: National Pension Service — 2026 employee rate and monthly income ceiling, National Health Insurance Service — 2026 health and long-term care rates, Korean Income Tax Act — wage deduction and tax rules, OECD — Korea 2026 tax and social contribution methodology.

Colombia model: 2026 resident employee estimate

The Colombia estimate assumes a resident employee with 12 equal monthly salaries and no other income or personal deductions. It applies employee health and pension contributions of 4% each, plus the Pension Solidarity Fund contribution when monthly salary exceeds four minimum monthly wages. Annual employment income is reduced by employee social contributions and the 25% exempt labor-income deduction, capped at 790 UVT. The 2026 progressive income-tax brackets are applied in UVT, using the official 2026 UVT value of COP 52,374. The model excludes service bonuses, other annual payments, dependants, voluntary pension contributions, mortgage interest, prepaid health deductions and other personal reliefs; actual withholding may differ.

Official sources: DIAN — 2026 UVT value, DIAN — personal income tax guidance, Invest in Colombia — 2026 employee social-security contribution rates.

Ecuador model: 2026 resident employee estimate

The Ecuador estimate assumes a resident private-sector employee with 12 regular monthly salary payments. It applies the employee IESS contribution of 9.45% and deducts that contribution from annual employment income before applying the 2026 personal income-tax table published by the SRI. The 2026 tax brackets run from the USD 12,208 non-taxable band through progressive rates of 5%, 10%, 12%, 15%, 20%, 25%, 30%, 35% and 37%. The estimate excludes personal-expense rebates, family-related benefits, 13th/14th salary bonuses (which are treated separately), employer IESS contributions, reserve funds and other individual adjustments. As a result, actual annual tax may be lower when eligible personal-expense deductions are claimed.

Official sources: SRI — 2026 personal income-tax table, IESS — 9.45% employee contribution and employer share.

Peru model: 2026 fifth-category employee estimate

The Peru estimate assumes a resident private-sector employee enrolled in the public National Pension System (ONP). It projects 14 salary payments per year (12 regular salaries plus two statutory gratificaciones), deducts the annual 7 UIT allowance and applies the progressive employment-income rates of 8%, 14%, 17%, 20% and 30%. The 2026 UIT is S/ 5,500. The ONP employee contribution is 13% of ordinary monthly salary. The displayed net is an ordinary-month equivalent; the annual tax projection includes the two additional salary payments, whose actual payslip withholding can differ in the bonus months. EsSalud is employer-paid and is not deducted from employee pay. The model excludes private AFP alternatives, additional 3 UIT expense deductions, other income, and personal tax credits.

Official sources: SUNAT — 2026 UIT value, SUNAT — fifth-category annual income tax calculation and rates, Government of Peru / SUNAT — 13% ONP employee contribution.

Chile model: November 2026 employee payroll estimate

The Chile estimate uses the SII November 2026 monthly Second Category Tax table and UTM of CLP 72,440. It assumes an indefinite-term employment contract and AFP Uno’s published 0.46% commission, in addition to the mandatory 10% pension contribution. Health is 7% and the employee unemployment-insurance contribution is 0.6%. Pension and health contributions are capped at 90 UF; unemployment insurance is capped at 135.2 UF. The November 20, 2026 UF value of CLP 41,371.05 is used for the contribution caps. Employer contributions, voluntary savings, other AFP commission rates, private health plan differences and individual tax adjustments are excluded. UF values and tax thresholds change over time.

Official sources: SII — 2026 monthly Second Category Tax tables, Superintendencia de Pensiones — 2026 contribution caps, Superintendencia de Pensiones — AFP commission rates and employer insurance, AFC Chile — employee unemployment contribution, SII — daily UF values for 2026.

Mexico model: 2026 employee payroll estimate

The Mexico estimate uses the 2026 monthly ISR withholding tariff published in Annex 8 of the Miscellaneous Fiscal Resolution. It applies the 2026 employment subsidy of up to MXN 535.65 for monthly taxable salary not exceeding MXN 11,492.66. Employee IMSS contributions are estimated using a first-year integrated salary factor based on 15 days of year-end bonus, 12 vacation days and a 25% vacation premium. The employee rate includes 2.375% on the integrated contribution base plus 0.40% on the portion above three daily UMA, capped at 25 UMA. The 2026 daily UMA is MXN 117.31. Employer-only IMSS and INFONAVIT charges, state payroll tax, variable salary components, other benefits and individual adjustments are excluded. Actual IMSS bases depend on the employee’s integrated salary and work/payroll details.

Official sources: SAT — 2026 ISR withholding tariff, Annex 8, Diario Oficial — 2026 employment subsidy, IMSS — Social Security Law and employee contribution rules, INEGI — 2026 UMA value.

Canada model: 2026 Ontario employee estimate

The Canada estimate assumes an Ontario resident, single employee and employment income only for the 2026 calendar year. Federal income tax uses the 2026 federal brackets and indexed basic personal amount; Ontario income tax uses the 2026 Ontario brackets, basic personal credit, surtax, tax reduction and Ontario Health Premium. CPP includes the employee 5.95% contribution on pensionable earnings from CAD 3,500 to CAD 74,600 and CPP2 at 4% from CAD 74,600 to CAD 85,000. EI is 1.63% up to CAD 68,900. The model includes the federal Canada Employment Amount and basic CPP/EI credits. It excludes age, spouse, dependant and disability credits, union dues, RRSP deductions, employer benefits and other personal adjustments. Quebec and other provinces/territories are not represented.

Official sources: CRA — 2026 federal and provincial tax rates, CRA — 2026 Ontario payroll tax, credits and health premium, CRA — 2026 CPP, CPP2 and EI limits.

United States model: 2026 federal employee estimate

The US estimate assumes a single filer eligible for the 2026 federal standard deduction of US$16,100. It applies the 2026 federal income-tax brackets of 10%, 12%, 22%, 24%, 32%, 35% and 37%. Employee FICA includes Social Security at 6.2% on wages up to US$184,500, Medicare at 1.45% on all wages and Additional Medicare at 0.9% on wages above US$200,000. State and local income taxes are excluded because they vary by location; credits, health insurance, retirement contributions and other deductions are also excluded. The result is a federal-only estimate, not a complete state-specific take-home pay calculation.

Official sources: IRS Revenue Procedure 2025-32 — 2026 tax brackets and standard deduction, Social Security Administration — 2026 contribution rates and wage base, IRS — Social Security and Medicare withholding rates.

India model: Tax Year 2026–27 salaried employee estimate

The India estimate assumes a resident individual earning salary only and using the default new tax regime under Section 202 of the Income-tax Act, 2025. Gross salary is annualised over 12 months and reduced by the ₹75,000 standard deduction. Taxable income is taxed at 0% up to ₹4 lakh, then 5%, 10%, 15%, 20%, 25% and 30% across the statutory slabs up to and above ₹24 lakh. The Section 87A rebate is applied up to ₹12 lakh taxable income, with marginal relief immediately above that threshold. Surcharge is applied for taxable income above ₹50 lakh, with marginal relief at the ₹50 lakh and ₹1 crore thresholds; the 4% Health and Education Cess is then added. Employee provident-fund contributions, state professional tax, house-rent allowance, other exemptions, deductions, special-rate income and individual-specific reliefs are excluded. The model is an annualised estimate, not a monthly TDS statement.

Official sources: India Budget — Tax Year 2026–27 new-regime slabs, Income Tax Department — Budget 2026 FAQ and Section 87A rebate, Income Tax Department — ₹75,000 salary standard deduction.

Australia model: 2026/27 resident employee estimate

The Australia estimate assumes a resident employee for the full 2026–27 financial year (1 July 2026 to 30 June 2027), eligible for the full AUD 18,200 tax-free threshold. It applies the 2026–27 resident tax rates: 15% from AUD 18,201 to 45,000, 30% to 135,000, 37% to 190,000 and 45% above. The Medicare levy is 2%, phased in for a single taxpayer between the AUD 28,011 and AUD 35,013 low-income thresholds. The Medicare levy surcharge is excluded because private hospital cover and family status are not selected. Deductions, offsets, HELP/HECS repayments and employer superannuation contributions are excluded; superannuation is not an employee payroll deduction.

Official sources: Australian Taxation Office — resident income tax rates 2026–27, ATO — 2026 withholding schedules and Medicare levy phase-in thresholds, ATO — 2026–27 Medicare levy surcharge thresholds.

New Zealand model: 2026/27 PAYE employee estimate

The New Zealand estimate assumes a resident employee with one main job and a full tax year from 1 April 2026 to 31 March 2027. Annual gross salary is calculated from 12 equal monthly payments. PAYE income tax uses the individual tax brackets: 10.5% up to NZ$15,600, 17.5% to NZ$53,500, 30% to NZ$78,100, 33% to NZ$180,000 and 39% above. The ACC earners’ levy is 1.75% including GST, applied to liable earnings up to NZ$156,641. The model excludes the Independent Earner Tax Credit, student loan deductions, KiwiSaver contributions, secondary income, family credits and other personal adjustments. This is an indicative annualised estimate; actual PAYE withholding depends on tax code and pay frequency.

Official sources: Inland Revenue — individual income tax rates, Inland Revenue — 2026/27 ACC earners’ levy and cap, New Zealand Legislation — Accident Compensation (Earners’ Levy) Regulations.

Austria model: 2026 employee estimate

The Austria estimate assumes a standard employee outside Vienna, no children and 12 equal monthly salary payments. It applies the 2026 income-tax brackets (€13,539 tax-free; then 20%, 30%, 40%, 48%, 50% and 55%), the €132 annual employee expense allowance and the €496 annual Verkehrsabsetzbetrag. The 2026 low-income supplement to the Verkehrsabsetzbetrag is also included, phasing out between €19,761 and €30,259 of taxable income. Employee social insurance includes health (3.87%), pension (10.25%), unemployment (0–2.95% depending on monthly earnings), Chamber of Labour (0.50%) and housing-promotion contribution (0.50%), capped at €6,930 monthly. The employee contribution is not charged below the €551.10 marginal-employment threshold. Vienna has a higher housing-promotion contribution, and special 13th/14th salary payments receive separate tax treatment; both are excluded.

Official sources: Austrian government — 2026 income-tax brackets, Social Insurance — 2026 contribution rates and ceilings, Social Insurance — 2026 low-income unemployment contribution rates, BMF — 2026 employee tax credits.

Portugal model: 2026 mainland employee estimate

The Portugal estimate assumes a single mainland tax resident, one employment income, no dependants and no additional household expense credits. Monthly gross salary is annualised over 12 equal payments; 13th/14th salary payments, holiday pay and region-specific Madeira/Azores rules are excluded. Employee Social Security is 11% of gross salary. The employment-income specific deduction is the higher of €4,587.09 (8.54 × 2026 IAS) or mandatory employee social contributions, limited to gross income. Taxable income is calculated using the revised 2026 IRS brackets enacted by Decree-Law 212/2026, effective 5 November 2026: 12.20%, 15.20%, 20.70%, 23.60%, 30.60%, 34.60%, 43.10%, 44.60% and 48.00%. Additional solidarity tax applies to taxable income above €80,000. Household expense credits and individual deductions are excluded, so final annual tax may be lower.

Official sources: Diário da República — Decree-Law 212/2026 (updated 2026 IRS brackets), Portal das Finanças — CIRS Article 25 employment-income deduction, Government of Portugal — 11% employee Social Security contribution.

Ireland model: 2026 PAYE employee estimate

The Ireland estimate assumes a single PAYE employee with one employment, no pension contribution and no additional tax credits. It applies the 2026 single-person standard-rate band of €44,000, 20%/40% income-tax rates, the €2,000 Single Person Tax Credit and the €2,000 Employee PAYE Tax Credit. USC applies to the full annual income if it exceeds €13,000, using the 2026 bands of 0.5%, 2%, 3% and 8%. Class A employee PRSI uses the 2026 rates: 4.20% through 30 September and 4.35% from 1 October. The annualised estimate accounts for the 39 weeks at the earlier rate and 13 weeks at the later rate, including the weekly PRSI credit where applicable. Employer PRSI and optional pension deductions are excluded.

Official sources: Revenue — 2026 tax rates, bands and credits, Revenue — 2026 USC bands, Department of Social Protection — 2026 Class A PRSI rates.

Belgium model: 2026 white-collar employee estimate

The Belgium estimate assumes a single resident white-collar employee with no dependants. Monthly gross salary is annualised over 12 months; holiday pay and year-end bonus are excluded. Employee ONSS is calculated at 13.07%, reduced by the 2026 work bonus using the indexed September 2026 non-manual employee thresholds. Taxable employment income is reduced by the standard professional-expense allowance of 30%, capped at €6,070. The federal tax calculation applies the 2026 income-tax brackets (25%, 40%, 45% and 50%) and the basic personal tax allowance of €11,550. The model excludes the municipality-specific local tax surcharge, special social-security contribution, regional tax reductions, family allowances and any sector-specific payroll variations; actual net pay can therefore differ.

Official sources: FPS Finance — 2026 income-tax brackets and personal allowance, FPS Finance — employee professional-expense deduction, ONSS — employee social-security contribution rate, and 2026 work-bonus thresholds for non-manual employees.

Netherlands model: 2026 employee estimate

The Netherlands estimate assumes an employee below the AOW age, resident in the Netherlands for the full year, with one employment income and both the general tax credit and labour tax credit applied. It annualises monthly gross salary over 12 months and applies the 2026 Box 1 rates: 35.75% up to €38,883, 37.56% from €38,883 to €78,426, and 49.50% above €78,426. The first bracket combines income tax with national insurance premiums; these are included in the payroll-tax result rather than shown as a separate employee social-insurance deduction. The 2026 general tax credit and labour tax credit are calculated using the published income thresholds and phase-out rates. For a standard employee, the employer normally pays the income-related Zvw contribution (6.10% in 2026), so it is not deducted from take-home pay. Private basic health-insurance premiums are paid separately and are excluded.

Official sources: Belastingdienst — 2026 tax rates and tax credits, Belastingdienst — 2026 Zvw contribution rates.

Important information

Net salary depends on more than the country: tax year, tax class or filing status, region, age, children, health insurance, pension scheme and other deductions can all affect the result.

The available Germany, France, Italy, Spain, Netherlands, Belgium, Ireland, Portugal, Austria, Norway, Denmark, Sweden, Switzerland, Australia, Canada, Chile, Colombia, Ecuador, India, Mexico, New Zealand, Peru, South Korea, Uruguay, United States, Brazil and UK models are simplified estimates, not official payroll calculations. The other 3 country models are not yet enabled. Do not use this tool as tax, payroll or legal advice.
